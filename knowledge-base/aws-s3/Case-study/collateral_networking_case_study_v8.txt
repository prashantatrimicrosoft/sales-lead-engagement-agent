Title: Protecting Network Infrastructure from Power-Related Downtime
Doc_Type: Case Study
Industry_Tag: Electronics, Networking, Telecommunications

Summary:
A network equipment manufacturer was expanding its production and testing facilities, and a single unplanned power interruption during a firmware validation run could cost days of re-testing and delay equipment shipments to customers. Standard utility power alone wasn't reliable enough to protect facilities running continuous network testing and manufacturing equipment.

The company installed a standby generator system sized for its production floor and testing labs, paired with an ongoing SLA covering scheduled maintenance, load testing, and 24-hour emergency response. Since installation, the facility has avoided production downtime during three separate regional outages, and the maintenance contract has caught two potential generator issues before they became failures.

For electronics and networking companies specifically, where testing equipment and clean rooms are sensitive to even brief power interruptions, a properly sized standby generator with an active SLA isn't a backup plan - it's part of maintaining production quality and delivery schedules.

Why it matters: in equipment manufacturing, an hour of unplanned downtime often costs far more than a year of generator maintenance.
