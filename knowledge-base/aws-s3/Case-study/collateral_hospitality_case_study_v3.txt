Title: Keeping Guest Operations Running When the Power Doesn't
Doc_Type: Case Study
Industry_Tag: Hospitality, Hotels, Resorts

Summary:
A hotel and resort operator faced a different kind of downtime risk than most industries: a power outage isn't just an internal operational problem, it's immediately visible to paying guests. Dark hallways, non-functioning elevators, and a shut-down kitchen mid-service create complaints and refund requests, not just delays.

The property installed a standby generator sized to cover guest-critical loads - kitchen operations, elevators, emergency lighting, and climate control - separate from a smaller portable unit used for outdoor event power during planned functions. Paired with an SLA including pre-season load testing ahead of peak booking periods, the property maintained full guest services through two separate outages, with guests largely unaware an outage had even occurred.

For hospitality operators specifically, backup power planning has to account for two different scenarios: continuous protection for core guest-facing systems, and flexible, portable power for events and outdoor functions - which often calls for a different generator configuration than a typical industrial facility.

Why it matters: in hospitality, an outage doesn't just cost operating time - it costs guest trust, and often shows up in reviews.
